# TerraProof demonstration video script

**Target runtime:** 4 minutes 30 seconds, including short pauses and screen actions.  
**Language:** English; add English subtitles.  
**Team:** Replace `[TEAM NAME]` before recording.  
**Basis:** [Submission and video guidelines](../FINAL_Submission_and_Guidelines.md), [current requirements and submission brief](submission-brief.md), [product scope](../PRODUCT.md), and [implemented rotation rules](rotation-comparison.md).

Read the quoted text aloud. Screen directions and production notes are not spoken. The timings are editing targets; rehearse once to fit your delivery.

## 1. Introduction — 0:00–0:15 (15 seconds)

**Screen:** TerraProof title, team name, and the application globe. Subtitle: “Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data.”

> We are [TEAM NAME], and this is TerraProof. We help farmers and agricultural advisers explore what to grow next by bringing regional soil evidence, NASA weather data, and crop references into one practical planning workflow.

## 2. Problem — 0:15–0:45 (30 seconds)

**Screen:** Simple question: “What should I grow after this harvest?” Show soil, weather, calendar, and water icons, followed by the actual application.

> After a harvest, a farmer faces another decision: what should grow on this land next? Soil information, weather records, and crop requirements are often scattered across different sources. A crop that looks promising still needs the right planting window, available water, seeds, labor, and buyers. Farmers and advisers need a clearer starting point for comparing options and identifying what still needs checking locally.

## 3. Solution — 0:45–1:15 (30 seconds)

**Screen:** Show the three-step guide, then the land selection, soil report, and crop comparison areas. Add a small “Working local prototype” label.

> TerraProof connects those questions in three steps: find your land, understand the soil evidence, and compare crop plans. NASA POWER supplies historical temperature, rainfall, and wetness context. ISRIC SoilGrids supplies regional soil predictions, while FAO references describe crop requirements. Users compare their usual two-season sequence with two alternatives and download a plan for adviser discussion. This working prototype provides preliminary screening; agricultural validation remains future work.

## 4. Live demonstration — 1:15–3:15 (2 minutes)

### 1:15–1:35 — Find the land

**Screen action:** Start on the globe. Choose **Albay example**, zoom in, click **Draw a region**, and select two opposite corners. Show the selected rectangle.

> Let’s use the Albay example. I locate the land and draw a rectangle around the area to assess. TerraProof supports regions worldwide, subject to source availability. This selection defines our area of interest; it does not establish a surveyed farm boundary.

### 1:35–2:00 — Assess the soil

**Screen action:** Show the depth and wetness-date controls. Click **Assess selected region**. Once the response appears, expand soil acidity and show the NASA wetness section. Keep actual source and missing-data labels visible.

> I choose a soil depth and wetness end date, then assess the region. The report explains properties such as soil acidity, carbon, and texture. NASA POWER adds recent regional wetness and weather context. These are regional estimates, so a local soil test still matters. Missing information stays visible as unknown.

### 2:00–2:25 — Enter three crop plans

**Screen action:** Open **What could I grow after this crop?** Use these illustrative inputs: first crop **Rice (indica)**, June 2027; usual next crop **Rice (indica)**, December; alternatives **Soybean** and **Maize (grain)**. Set water to **Not sure yet** and priority to **Understand water needs**. Click **Compare my three crop plans**.

> Now I enter an example: rice in June, followed by rice in December. I choose soybean and maize as alternative next crops. These are demonstration choices, not local recommendations. I leave water availability unconfirmed and ask to understand water needs. Then I compare all three plans using the same first crop.

### 2:25–2:55 — Interpret the actual results

**Screen action:** Show the returned comparison cards. Point to their actual status labels, planting and harvest dates, water explanations, and local checks. Do not replace the displayed result with a prewritten outcome.

> The results check soil acidity, historical seasonal temperature, and possible calendar overlaps. “Worth exploring” means those limited checks passed. “Needs checking” flags missing or borderline evidence. “Potential conflict” flags a reference-range or timing issue. Harvest windows and water-use references support discussion, but they do not predict yield or calculate irrigation liters. Seeds, drainage, labor, and buyers still need local checks.

### 2:55–3:15 — Download the plan

**Screen action:** Click **Download comparison** and open the downloaded text file. Show its three sequences and adviser checklist at a readable size.

> Finally, I download the comparison. It keeps the three crop sequences, indicative harvest windows, explanations, and next steps together. The farmer can bring this readable plan to an adviser and discuss the unresolved questions before making a substantial change.

## 5. Features — 3:15–4:00 (45 seconds)

**Screen:** Use short captures of actual working screens: a property explanation and source details, the seven crop profiles, the browser notebook, and the downloaded plan. Finish with a source card distinguishing NASA from other sources.

> TerraProof combines a global map, understandable soil explanations, seven crop-reference profiles, and two-season comparisons. Users can inspect sources, download evidence, and keep plots, management notes, and laboratory pH records in a browser notebook. NASA POWER provides numerical environmental context; NASA GIBS imagery supports globe navigation. SoilGrids and FAO provide additional, non-NASA evidence. The comparison uses documented rules. It screens crops independently and does not simulate how one crop changes the next season’s soil or water. Its contribution is making evidence and remaining questions easier to review together.

## 6. Impact — 4:00–4:30 (30 seconds)

**Screen:** Farmer-to-adviser workflow, followed by an end card: “Find your land. Understand the evidence. Discuss what comes next.” Include TerraProof and the team name.

> TerraProof aims to help farmers and advisers turn scattered information into better-informed conversations about the next crop. Its intended value is clearer comparisons and more explicit questions before committing land, labor, and money. We have not yet measured gains in yield, water savings, or soil health. Our next steps are local crop review, farmer testing, and independent field validation. TerraProof: understand the evidence, then plan what comes next.

- The notebook stores records in the browser. If showing it, use fictional demonstration notes and avoid implying shared accounts or cloud synchronization.
- Record the current local build. Label any added future-feature mock-up explicitly; the scripted main workflow uses implemented features.
- Keep labels, units, dates, source attributions, and limitations readable. Exclude credentials and personal information. Use original or appropriately licensed visuals and music.
